`librefang security audit-reanchor` recovers from a broken audit hash chain without discarding forensic history.
The only existing tool, `audit-reset`, unconditionally wipes the entire `audit_entries` table, destroying every entry including the ones before the break that verified cleanly.
`audit-reanchor` diagnoses the first break (a `prev_hash` link mismatch or a content `hash` mismatch) against the raw database, reports the exact seq with the expected and found values, then — with `--confirm` — truncates only the broken entry and anything after it and writes a `ChainReanchored` marker so the chain resumes from the last entry that verified.
The daemon's pre-existing behaviour of logging the break and starting anyway rather than aborting now has regression coverage too.
The root cause of the break itself was not determined: the concurrent-writer race is blocked by the `seq INTEGER PRIMARY KEY` constraint (a colliding `INSERT` fails closed rather than corrupting the chain) and the existing write-ordering hardening (the tip only advances after a durable commit) rules out death mid-write, so confirming the actual trigger needs host logs spanning the broken write (#7747) (@DaBlitzStein)
