`librefang security audit-reanchor` recovers from a broken audit hash chain (e.g. the "chain break at seq 204" reported on proteo) without discarding forensic history.
The daemon already logged the exact seq, expected hash, and found hash on boot and kept starting rather than aborting — that detection and degrade-not-crash behavior is now covered by regression tests.
What was missing was recovery: the only existing tool, `audit-reset`, unconditionally wipes the entire `audit_entries` table, destroying every entry including the ones before the break that verified cleanly.
`audit-reanchor` diagnoses the first break (`prev_hash` link mismatch or content `hash` mismatch) against the raw database, then — with `--confirm` — truncates only the broken entry and anything after it and writes a `ChainReanchored` marker so the chain resumes from the last entry that verified, preserving every entry before the break.
Root cause of the specific proteo break was not determined from the code: the concurrent-daemon write race the original report suspected is blocked by the `seq INTEGER PRIMARY KEY` constraint (a colliding writer's `INSERT` fails closed and the entry is dropped rather than corrupting the chain), and the existing single-process write-ordering hardening (tip only advances after a durable commit) rules out the other obvious mechanism.
Confirming the actual trigger needs the proteo host's daemon/journal logs spanning the seq-204 write and any out-of-band operations (binary swaps, backups, restores) touching `~/.librefang/librefang.db` around that time (#7702) (@DaBlitzStein)
